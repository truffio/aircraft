# C2 — Interface needs (FU), rework for G2 and D69

What the FU requirements (`work/C2/L2_FU.md`, reworked) need from EF (C3), DYN and MS-DYN (C1) and the TST, and what FU gives to DYN, EF, FC, AF, FCP and TM. Interface IDs I-nn are those of FUNCTIONAL_ANALYSIS §3.2 as changed by CR-B3-39 (D73). "Boundary ICD" is the vehicle-model boundary ICD, whose home I1 decides (IN-B1-01, TT-720). C2 does not coordinate with other agents directly; I1 resolves these. IDs are never reused: the needs answered since the first draft keep their ID with a status, and the rework adds IN-C2-19, IN-C2-24 and IN-C2-50 to -54.

## 1. Summary of the FU interfaces

| Interface | Direction | Data (SI) | Rate | Defined in | FU record |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I-01 | EF → FU | Control force `F_c^B` and control moment `M_c^B` about the centre of gravity, computed once by EF from its effector outputs (DD-190) | Each simulation step, held over the step | MS-DYN | L2-AER-FU-070 |
| I-03 | FU → EF, DYN (ground contact) | Fuel availability (available / not available) | Each simulation step | MS-DYN | L2-AER-FU-200, -320 |
| I-04 | FU → DYN | Vehicle mass (kg); inertia tensor as three principal moments (kg·m², body axes, zero products). **No centre-of-gravity item** (G2-F05) | Each simulation step | MS-DYN | L2-AER-FU-120 |
| I-05 | FU → FC | Mass properties: vehicle mass, centre-of-gravity position (the body-frame origin), inertia tensor | Each step; read at the first step of each control frame | Boundary ICD | L2-AER-FU-130, -140, -150 |
| I-06 | FU → AF, FCP | Fuel state: remaining fuel mass (kg), low-fuel indication, fuel-exhaustion indication | Each step; read at the first step of each control frame | Boundary ICD; ICD-FCP refers to it | L2-AER-FU-130, -210 to -240 |
| I-21 | TST → FU | Fuel mass of the initial condition (kg), for a ground start or an airborne test start; vehicle parameter set | Start of run | ICD-AER-TST | L2-AER-FU-010, -020, -030, -330 |
| (new) | FU → TST | Initial-condition error naming the item (initial fuel mass, or an inertia item of the vehicle parameter set) and its value | Start of run | ICD-AER-TST | L2-AER-FU-030, -330 |
| I-31 | FU → TM | Fuel mass flow (kg/s), vehicle mass (kg), remaining fuel mass (kg) | Each step; sampled at each output sample | ICD-AER-TST | L2-AER-FU-250, -260 |

FU takes nothing from the flight software (neither the effector commands nor the landing gear command), nothing from the flight control panel and nothing from the landing gear: the gear has no mass and changes no mass property (L1-AER-900, L2-AER-FU-310). FU does not use the per-effector outputs.

| FC (C4) | Mass properties of each step, the same vehicle mass that DYN uses (L2-AER-FU-140) | Boundary ICD (I-05) | **IN-C2-41 (corrected, G2-F24).** FC reads the mass at the frame start; within one control frame the mass falls by the fuel used in that frame. The relative fall is at most `(k_t·‖F_c‖ + k_r·‖M_c‖)·[P-SYS-310]/m`. The earlier bound assumed `‖F_c‖/m` within the crew limit, which is false at high drag. A bound from the authority instead: with the C3 reference layout (six effectors of 100 kN at moment arms of 1.5 m and 4 m), `k_t·Σ|u_i| + k_r·Σ|u_i|·|r_i| ≤ 2.34e-7 × (6e5 + 1.4e6) = 0.47 kg/s`, so at dry mass the fall over 0.02 s is at most 9.4e-6 of the mass (about 1.6e-6 for the largest resultant force alone, and 7.7e-7 in the inspector's case of 300 kt into the largest wind). The conclusion stands: negligible for the crew limit. Maximum inertia for the moment budget `[P-FU-070] + [P-FU-080]` = (2850, 15975, 15975) kg·m² (full fuel); minimum `[P-FU-070]` (exhaustion). On the ground with the effectors off the fuel is constant (L2-AER-FU-300). |
