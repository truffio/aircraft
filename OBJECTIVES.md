# Aeronef Project — Objectives

**Date:** October 24, 2026

## 1. Purpose

This project is a controlled systems-engineering experiment using AI to design, test, and reverse engineer a fictional flying vehicle, referred to as the Aeronef. The goal is not to build a realistic commercial aircraft, but to create a sufficiently rich, fully understood system for evaluating AI-assisted engineering methods across the complete lifecycle.

The project has three principal objectives:

1. Design the Aeronef and its flight-control and autoflight systems.
2. Develop a scalable simulation and automated verification environment.
3. Independently reverse engineer the resulting system using operational documentation and observable behavior, without access to its original design.

## 2. Objective 1 — Design the Aeronef

Define a deliberately simplified fictional vehicle and its behavior from the system, flight-control, and autoflight perspectives.

The initial Aeronef will have a simple shape, provisionally resembling a Tic Tac, with an identifiable nose and cockpit. It will have six degrees of freedom: translation along three axes and rotation about three axes. Motion will be generated by fictional force emitters rather than aerodynamic control surfaces or conventional propulsion. The design will initially avoid coupling between axes, while representing gravity, air resistance, mass, and simplified moments of inertia.

A finite supply of fictional, high-energy-density fuel will power the force emitters. Fuel consumption will affect vehicle mass, using configurable consumption relationships for linear and rotational actuation. The center of gravity will initially be treated as fixed, with deliberately simplified mass and inertia assumptions.

Autoflight will be the primary means of controlling the vehicle, since direct manual control may be impractical. The system will adapt selected flight guidance and autoflight modes from commercial aviation to the unique physics of the Aeronef. Different flight-control laws and autoflight implementations will be developed and compared.

## 3. Objective 2 — Build the Simulator and Automated Test Environment

Develop a simulation environment capable of executing the Aeronef's behavior and evaluating it against defined requirements. A graphical interface is optional; the core simulator must operate independently of graphics.

The environment will support two complementary uses:

- **Interactive testing**, allowing a human to apply commands, observe responses, and explore system behavior.
- **Automated testing**, in which procedures specify conditions and actions, execute simulations, collect results, and assess observed outcomes against expected outcomes.

Any test procedure—not only Monte Carlo experiments—must be executable in independent, parallel simulation instances across available CPU resources. This includes functional and logic tests, mode-transition tests, performance tests, and statistical campaigns. The test platform will support batch execution, scalable parallelism, repeatable scenarios, and comparison of alternative control laws and autoflight versions.

Results must be collected in a consistent format and presented in human-readable reports, with sufficient data to understand outcomes and investigate deviations.

## 4. Objective 3 — Reverse Engineer the System

Evaluate whether independent AI agents can reconstruct the Aeronef's functional rules and control logic from evidence rather than from its original engineering specifications.

The reverse-engineering effort will not have access to the original requirements, design documents, blueprints, or implementation. Instead, it will use operational documents written for a human audience—such as user manuals and operating procedures—together with observations of the system in simulation and data collected during testing.

The Aeronef's flight-control and autoflight systems will expose operational data that make it possible to correlate inputs, actions, mode changes, and observed vehicle responses. This evidence will help reverse-engineering agents infer cause-and-effect relationships and reconstruct underlying behavior, including autoflight logic and mode transitions.

The independently inferred model will then be compared with the original design and requirements by the project evaluators to assess the accuracy, coverage, and limitations of the reverse-engineering methods. Separation between original design information and the material available to reverse-engineering agents is essential to the experiment.

## 5. Documentation and Systems-Engineering Approach

Develop the project through a hierarchical, requirements-driven process:

- **Level 0 (L0):** overall mission and project objectives.
- **Level 1 (L1):** major system elements and responsibilities, including the Aeronef, simulation and testing platform, and reverse-engineering effort.
- **Lower levels:** progressively refined requirements and subfunctions, using as many levels as are useful for each element rather than imposing a fixed depth.

Produce documented engineering artifacts and subject them to peer review. Maintain traceability between parent and child requirements and between requirements and verification procedures. In addition to design documentation, produce human-oriented operational manuals and procedures that can serve as evidence for the reverse-engineering exercise.

## 6. Overall Success Criteria

The experiment should demonstrate the ability to:

- Design a coherent fictional vehicle with defined dynamics, control laws, and autoflight behavior.
- Verify both behavior and performance through repeatable, automatically assessed tests, including large-scale parallel campaigns.
- Compare alternative implementations under equivalent test conditions.
- Reconstruct meaningful aspects of the system's behavior and logic from manuals and observed data, without access to the original design.
- Maintain a reviewed, traceable chain from objectives through requirements, design artifacts, tests, and findings.

The intended outcome is both a functioning Aeronef model and a practical assessment of AI-assisted system design, automated verification, and evidence-based reverse engineering.
