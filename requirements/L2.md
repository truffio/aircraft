## DYN — Dynamics model

The dynamics model requirements (task C1). The subject of each statement is the dynamics model, the DYN subsystem of the vehicle model (glossary *Dynamics model*). The physics is stated in [specs/MS-DYN.md](../specs/MS-DYN.md); these records point to its named sections, because a statement cannot carry a section number. DYN implements functions F-AER-03, F-AER-04, F-AER-12, F-AER-15 and its part of F-AER-14 (FUNCTIONAL_ANALYSIS §6.1 as revised by CR-B3-38, CR-B3-40).

**Depth.** Each record below is a leaf: the next layer would be the implementation (integration method, code structure), so DYN has no L3 section (D4). Each record is verified directly by its own method (D40). Procedure IDs follow the record number (IN-, AN-, TP-, DM-DYN-nnn); Phase 5 (V1) writes the procedures.

**Owner answers.** D64: the rotation-during-translation term is retained (#81.1, option T1) and #81 is closed with the MS-DYN kinematic terms list; drag is isotropic quadratic with no aerodynamic moment (option D1); the gust shape and the 2 s rise time, the capsule and its dimensions (TBR) and standard gravity (Set) are accepted. D69 and D73 (the approved B3 package, L1-AER-900 to -970 and CR-B3-01 to -41): a retractable landing gear, a rest on the ground under a ground reaction, runs that start on the ground (airborne starts for test runs and credibility checks only, B3-1), and any contact other than a landing on the extended gear within the envelope with fuel remaining is a crash (B3-3). D72: no long checks (the torque-free and rotation-during-translation checks run gravity-free over [P-DYN-270]); isotropic drag meets D18; [P-SYS-230] is a ground speed. This version 2 applies the G2 findings and D69; `work/C1/rework_G2_D69.md` gives the disposition of each finding and each change.

**Coverage of the L1 parents.** Every L1 record allocated to DYN has at least one DYN child. L1-AER-230 is Deleted (D54) and has none. The L1 records cited are those of AER-L1-baseline-1 as changed by CR-B3-01 to -41, with the new records L1-AER-900 to -970 (D73).

### L2-AER-DYN-140

```req
ID:            L2-AER-DYN-140
Level:         L2
Type:          Model fidelity
Statement:     The dynamics model shall compute the air-relative velocity of each simulation step as
  the vehicle velocity minus the wind velocity that the TST gives with the command for that step.
Rationale:     The wind of the run, steady wind plus gusts within the wind envelope, comes with each
  step command (L1-AER-670, I-25, D51, D62). The model uses it only through the air-relative
  velocity. The wind envelope itself, including the gust shape and the rise time, is defined in
  the MS-DYN wind model; the TST applies only winds inside it. The test of the air-relative velocity
```

## FU — Fuel and mass properties

The fuel and mass-properties requirements (task C2; reworked for the G2 findings and the landing gear, D69, D72, D73: `work/C2/rework_G2_D69.md`). The subject of each statement is the FU subsystem, the fuel and mass-properties subsystem of the Aeronef, one of the three subsystems of the vehicle model, with the dynamics model (DYN) and the effector subsystem (EF), behind the vehicle-model boundary (D55, L1-AER-430). FU runs at each simulation step `[P-SYS-300]`. Its inputs are the fuel mass of the initial condition, the vehicle parameter set and the control force and control moment that EF computes from its effector outputs at each step (DD-C3-05). Its outputs are the fuel mass flow, the remaining fuel mass, the mass properties (vehicle mass, centre-of-gravity position, inertia tensor), the fuel availability for the effectors and the dynamics model, and the fuel state. FU takes nothing from the landing gear: the gear has no mass and moves no mass property (L1-AER-900).

**What FU decides.** The consumption relationships are linear in the magnitude of the resultant control force and of the resultant control moment (DD-110, owner D65), so the fuel mass flow is zero whenever the effectors produce nothing: on the ground with the effectors off (L1-AER-930, -940), and after fuel exhaustion. The inertia tensor changes with the fuel: it runs linearly from the dry-mass tensor at fuel exhaustion to its full-capacity value, because the fuel depletes uniformly over a fixed volume centred on the centre of gravity (DD-120, D65), taken equal to the body shape (DD-C2-07, proposed). In the exhaustion step the flow is limited to the fuel left, and the remaining fuel mass is set to zero at the end of the step (DD-130, revised by DD-C2-06, proposed). The equations go in the MS-DYN mass-properties model and fuel model, which C1 merges; `work/C2/ms_dyn_s6_s7_fu.md` gives the text.

**Exhaustion is nominal.** Fuel exhaustion is nominal behaviour, not a failure (L0-ASM-070). FU then gives the dry-mass properties, zero fuel mass flow, a fuel availability of not available and the exhaustion indication, and it keeps doing so to the end of the run. In flight, the vehicle then falls, and its ground contact is a crash (D68, L1-AER-270); DYN classifies the contact with the fuel availability that FU gives it. A run that starts on the ground with no fuel stays on the ground.

**Ground operations (D69).** Runs start on the ground, at rest on the extended landing gear with the effectors off (L1-AER-950), and take their fuel mass from the initial condition as before. A run no longer ends at the first ground contact: a landing is followed by a rest on the ground with a constant remaining fuel mass, and the run ends at a crash or at the TST's end condition. The hover endurance now counts from a takeoff at full fuel (L1-AER-360 v2), and the fuel of the takeoff lies inside the margin `[P-FU-100]` (L2-AER-FU-290).

**Verification.** Each record is a leaf at L2 (`Allocated to: —`): the next layer would describe the implementation (D4), so each record is verified directly by its own method. The L1 parents are verified by roll-up (D40, D58). Several tests read FU outputs that the operational data do not carry (the centre of gravity, the inertia tensor, the fuel availability) or need each simulation step; they read them at the vehicle-model boundary or at the interfaces of MS-DYN, recorded by the TST for the test run (IN-C2-30). The `Parameters` field of each record lists the parameters that its statement and its success criteria use, and the `TBD/TBR` field lists the open ones among them (G2-F20).

## EF — Effectors

The effector requirements (task C3). The subject of each statement is the effector subsystem (EF), one of the three subsystems of the vehicle model with the dynamics model (DYN) and the FU subsystem, behind the vehicle-model boundary (D55, L1-AER-430). EF implements function F-AER-01 (FUNCTIONAL_ANALYSIS §6.1). It runs at each simulation step `[P-SYS-300]`. Its inputs are the effector commands of the current control frame (one command per effector and the effector activation state, from flight control across the vehicle-model boundary), the fuel availability (from FU) and the vehicle parameter set. Its outputs are the effector output of each effector and the control force and control moment about the centre of gravity that they produce, given to DYN, to FU and, for the outputs, to the operational data.

**The effector model.** The physics is stated in the **effector model** of MS-DYN (section 7.1, proposed in `work/C3/ms_dyn_s7_effectors.md`); these records point to it by name, because a statement cannot carry a section number. In short: each effector is a force emitter along a fixed unit direction through a fixed point of application in the body frame (DD-150); its command is clipped to its output limits and followed through a first-order lag of time constant `[P-EF-020]` (DD-160); its output is zero while fuel is not available or the effectors are off (DD-180); and EF sums the outputs, once per step, into the one control force and control moment that DYN and FU both use (DD-190). Nothing else defines an effector (L0-CON-080, L1-AER-100).

**Solution-free.** The number of effectors and their layout are a design decision, not a requirement. The records constrain the effector set only through what it must achieve (the authority records L2-AER-EF-300 to -330 and the response record -340) and through the closed list of what defines an effector (-010). A reference layout that meets every record is proposed as DD-200 with TBR values (`work/C3/param_proposals.md`); replacing it is a parameter change, with no change to these records.

**Ground operations (D69, D73).** The effector commands carry an effector activation state, on or off, set by the flight software. While it is off, each effector output is zero, so the control force, the control moment and, under DD-110, the fuel mass flow are zero: this is how the effectors are off on the ground before Takeoff and after Land. EF does not model the landing gear. The off state is the EF share of L1-AER-930 (effectors off on the ground, B3, D73), so L2-AER-EF-210 traces to it; the design decision is DD-180.

**Verification.** Each record is a leaf at L2 (`Allocated to: —`): the next layer would describe the implementation (D4), so each record is verified directly by its own method, and the L1 parents are verified by roll-up (D40, D58). Procedure IDs follow the record number (IN-, AN-, TP-, DM-EF-nnn); Phase 5 (V1) writes the procedures. Several analyses read the effector commands, the effector outputs and the control force and moment at every simulation step; the TST records the vehicle-model interfaces at each step for those runs, as for FU (IN-C2-30, IN-C3-31). The tests that drive EF with scripted effector commands use a test command source in place of flight control (IN-C3-33).

**Coverage of the L1 parents.** Every live L1 record allocated to EF has at least one EF child, except L1-AER-960 and -970 (takeoff and landing capability), added by B3 (D73) after this section was drafted: their EF children are for the C3 rework. L1-AER-230 is Deleted (D54) and has none.
